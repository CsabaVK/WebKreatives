# Product

## Register

brand

## Client

Formasons Estates, trading as FGL Facilities (also FGL Cleaning, FGL London,
FGL Cardiff). Facilities management and property services for commercial
property in London and the surrounding area, with a second base in Cardiff.
Office: 1 Kingdom Street, Paddington, London W2 6BD. Phone 0330 043 3012
(alternate 0121 798 1329), info@formasons.com. Free consultation, fully licensed
and insured, 24-hour support line.

Six service families, each with named lines:

- Cleaning: office, pub and restaurant, jet wash and specialist, clearances and
  end-of-tenancy, commercial.
- Maintenance: planned, mechanical and electrical, grounds, general.
- Security: manned guarding, door supervision, key holding, vacant property
  supervision, concierge and CCTV.
- Handyman: general DIY, odd jobs, painting and decorating, locksmith.
- Washroom: supplies, equipment rental, service and maintenance, sanitary.
- Waste management: complete waste solution, general recycling, bins and skips.

Stated process: property analysis, customised plan, on-site consultation,
estimate, delivery, finishing touches. Stated values: complete care,
sustainable practice, 24-hour support, "excellence is in our DNA", a
satisfaction guarantee. Careers page collects applications (name, contact,
right to work in the UK, ID, CV) with no listed vacancies.

Recognition shown on the current site, to keep as facts (redesigned, not as
image badges): "Best of Home Org 2018 Winner", "100 remodelers West Coast",
"HoM Top Choices", "Mel8 Certified", plus "Award Winning Service". Three client
testimonials to keep verbatim: Mx Jacks Mayfield, Peter Scranford, Tom Jones
(all on property managed to "the highest standards", one for over a year, one
for several months).

Current site: a Wix template with a stock tunnel photo, badge images from the
theme, a "Thank you for contacting us" heading shown before anyone has written,
and "legacy" prose. The one strong asset is the mark: a fleur-de-lis built from
a chevron, two scrolls and a spear point, in deep green-black, with a Didone
wordmark. This build is a demo: forms show a success state, no endpoint.

## Users

Two readers, both on a work laptop during office hours, some on a phone between
site visits.

1. The office, building or estate manager for a commercial property (offices,
   pubs and restaurants, retail, managed residential blocks, vacant units) in
   London or Cardiff. They hold a budget, they are comparing three or four
   providers, and they want to know: which services can one contract cover, do
   they handle the awkward ones (vacant property, EOT clearance, out-of-hours
   security), will someone answer at 2 a.m., are they insured, and how fast can a
   quote come back.
2. A landlord or letting agent with one or a few properties who needs a
   reliable trade for turnovers: end-of-tenancy clean, small repairs, locks,
   waste. They want a phone number and a person, not a portal.

Both are put off by template sites and inflated "legacy" prose. They trust
specific lines of service, a real address, a real phone number, and a clear
next step.

## Product Purpose

Turn a comparison into a consultation request. The site should make one
contract for everything feel like the obvious answer: every service line
visible and named, the 24-hour promise and the insurance stated once and
plainly, and a quote request that takes one minute. Success is a submitted
consultation form or a call to 0330 043 3012 from any section of the page.

## Brand Personality

Stately. Exact. Present.

An estate steward, not a marketing department. The voice is short, formal
without being stiff, in British English: "We look after the building so you can
look after the business." It names things (end-of-tenancy clean, key holding,
skip hire) rather than praising itself, and where it makes a promise it makes a
concrete one (24-hour line, free consultation, licensed and insured). No "legacy
of excellence", no "tapestry of tradition", no "impeccable". The mark carries the
heritage; the copy carries the facts.

## Anti-references

- The current site: Wix template, badge images pasted in a row, stock photo
  of a tunnel, "A Legacy of Excellence", "Thank you for contacting us" shown
  before contact.
- Big FM consultancies (Mitie, CBRE, ISS): corporate blue, stock photos of
  smiling people in hi-vis, "solutions" and "integrated" everywhere, walls of
  equal icon tiles.
- Local cleaner and handyman sites: bright yellow and blue, star ratings in the
  hero, before-and-after sliders, "Call now!!!".
- Generic templates: purple or blue gradient hero, three equal cards with icons
  in circles, glass blur, everything centred, "Welcome to".

## Design Principles

1. **One contract, six services, all named.** The service list is the hero's
   partner, not a subpage. Every line is visible and readable on the home page;
   depth lives in `services/`.
2. **The mark is the guide.** The fleur-de-lis is the only ornament on the page
   and it moves: it hops from section to section as the reader scrolls,
   settling beside whatever they are reading. It replaces every other decorative
   element and every icon.
3. **Promises are sentences with numbers.** "Answered 24 hours a day." "Quote
   within one working day." "Fully licensed and insured." Never adjectives.
4. **Stone, ink and green.** The palette is a Paddington office building:
   Portland stone ground, near-black green type, one green accent that is the
   mark's own colour. Nothing else.
5. **Real London.** Photography is the client's own or nothing; until they
   supply it the page stands on type, the mark and layout. No stock tunnels, no
   stock hi-vis.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. British English. Body 17px minimum on phones. The moving mark respects
`prefers-reduced-motion` (rendered still, beside the current section, no hop)
and never covers text. All service names are text, never images. Tap targets
44px. Forms have visible labels and a plain success state.
